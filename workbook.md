# Workbook

The chapters explain the methods. These pages help you prepare. Each one lists the questions to answer before you open the assistant, and the checks to make after it answers. Answer them in your own words, and explaining the job to any assistant becomes easy. Copy a page into a notebook or a notes app, or print it, and fill in only what matters. More information isn't automatically better information.

Before you share anything with an AI tool, remove passwords, full account numbers, identity numbers, private medical identifiers and details another person didn't agree to share. Replace them with plain labels, such as *the electricity company* or *my son's school*. Keep the original somewhere safe.

## 1. Your next task (Chapters 1 and 2)

Write the situation in one sentence. *I need to ____.*

Before you ask
- What happened, and what result do I need in my hands at the end?
- What will I do with that result?
- Which facts are certain, and which are still unknown?
- What must not change?
- Which source document can I rely on?
- Would it help to be interviewed first, one question at a time?

After it answers
- Did it restate my problem correctly, and keep the unknowns as unknowns?
- Is there one small next step I can take today?
- What one correction will I make after the first attempt, and what will I keep for next time?

## 2. A clear request, and repairing one (Chapters 2 to 4)

- The help I want, such as an editor, a tutor, an organizer or a checklist maker.
- My situation, who is involved and what I already have.
- The limits, such as length, tone, deadline, budget, privacy and which sources may be used.
- The result, what the answer must contain and how I will know it is usable.

When the first answer misses
- Was it too broad, for the wrong reader, missing a source, in the wrong tone, built on an assumption or without a next step?
- Which one thing will I fix first, and what must stay exactly as it was?
- If I asked twice and got two answers, what fact or source would settle it?

## 3. Your Personal Context File (Chapter 5)

Five to ten lines that are true for most of your tasks, and nothing private.
- Who I am, in one line, and the work I do.
- How I like answers set out.
- The standing rules for my regular jobs, such as *amounts with two decimals, never rounded*.
- What the assistant must never assume.
- Where this file lives, and the date I last read it.

After a month, delete any line you never needed, and add any rule you had to repeat.

## 4. From document to action (Chapters 8 and 12)

- Who sent it, and on what date?
- What is the deadline, and who is affected?
- What action is required, and what is optional?
- Are there fees, and how do I make contact?
- Which words don't I understand, and is any part unreadable?

After it answers
- Can I find every requirement in the original, with its page or section?
- Is every deadline still there, and are conflicts and unreadable parts marked?
- What must I confirm with the organization myself?

## 5. Comparing options (Chapter 15)

- What is the decision, and by when?
- Which requirements are must-haves?
- Which facts will I record for every option, such as price, repeating costs, term, limits, cancellation and source?
- What is still unknown for each one, and which single fact would change the answer?

After it answers
- Was every option that fails a must-have removed, with a reason?
- Are facts, calculations and judgments kept apart?
- Does each remaining option have its main risk and one question to ask?

## 6. Preparing for an appointment (Chapter 11)

- What changed, and when did it begin?
- What makes it better or worse?
- Which measurements, medicines and allergies are relevant?
- Which three questions matter most to me?

After it answers
- Is my timeline correct, and are my observations kept apart from interpretations?
- Is there no diagnosis, no ranking of conditions and no suggestion to change treatment?

## 7. Checking a suspicious message (Chapter 14 and the Five-Minute Shield)

- Who sent it, and what does it ask me to do?
- What link or attachment does it contain? Don't open it.
- What pressure does it use, such as urgency, secrecy, fear or a prize?
- Which details can I check through an official channel I find myself?
- Does my family have a code word, and does everyone know it?

After it answers
- Did it point out the warning signs without calling the message definitely safe or definitely a scam?
- Did it suggest a safe way to check, without using any number or link in the message?

## 8. Planning a day or a week (Chapter 16)

- What are my fixed commitments, and which tasks are flexible?
- How much travel, preparation and rest does each one need?
- Where is my spare block for the unexpected?
- What does done mean for my three most important tasks?

After it answers
- Are the fixed times untouched, and are conflicts pointed out?
- Is there a lighter plan for a hard day, and a first action for tomorrow morning?

## 9. Drafting a message or a piece of writing (Chapters 9 and 17)

- Who is it for, and what must it achieve?
- Which facts must stay exactly as they are?
- What one action am I asking for, and by when?
- What tone fits this relationship?

After it answers
- Does it say what I mean, and is the request still there?
- Has a promise, a statistic or a story appeared that I didn't supply?
- Read it aloud. Would I say it?

## 10. Handing over to a person (Chapters 7 and 11)

- What is the goal, and what is the timeline so far?
- Which evidence and calculations matter, and on what assumptions?
- Which decisions have already been made?
- What exactly do I need the expert to confirm?

After it answers
- Does the one-page summary contain only the personal details that are truly needed?

## 11. The Permission Ladder (Chapter 22)

For one job, mark the highest rung it needs, and what you will check before it climbs.
- Look. It reads, and changes nothing.
- Suggest. It proposes, and you decide.
- Prepare. It makes a draft or a copy you can throw away.
- Change. It changes your own files, inside one folder, with a dated copy beside it.
- Send. It reaches other people. You approve each one.
- Act. It moves money, books, buys or deletes. You do this yourself.

The higher the rung, the more you check before, and the easier the undo must be.
- The highest rung this job needs.
- The rungs that stay closed.
- How I will undo the highest one.

## 12. Briefing an agent (Chapters 23 and 24)

- Which one folder may it work in, and is anything private in it?
- Where is the dated copy, and have I checked that it opens?
- What must it never do, such as delete, touch anything outside the folder, send, publish or install?
- In which order should it work? Look, show the plan and the full list, wait for my approval, then act.
- Where do unwanted files go, and where is every change recorded?
- Where is the canary file, and how will I check the result in the folder myself?
- Which mode will I set before my first message?

After it acts
- Do the counts match, before and after?
- Do five spot checks and three opened files agree with its report?
- Is the canary untouched?

## 13. Your kitchen folder (Chapter 26)

- The folder's name, named for the work.
- The seven rooms. Inbox, Output, Problems, Archive, Tools, Rules and Sessions.
- The instructions file, in five lines. The folder, one never, one always, a naming rule and *show me the list before any bulk change*.
- The work record. What is planned, in progress and done, with a date and an author on every line.
- The start-up check. What it looks at before work begins.
- The rhythm. Check, read, work, record, note.

The test
- A fresh session, given only the folder, can say what happens next, which file to use and what it must not change.

## 14. Designing an automation (Chapters 25, 27 and 28)

- Trigger. When does it run?
- Job. What runs, and was it tested by hand?
- Record. Where does the log live?
- Signal. How will I hear about a failure?
- Heartbeat. Where does it say *I ran*, and when do I look?
- Strange days. Empty week, the same item twice, a run stopped halfway, a changed file.
- Pause. How do I stop it?
- Owner. Who looks after it, and who else can pause it?
- Which actions need my approval every time?

Before turning it on
- Have I dropped one bad file in on purpose, and switched the schedule off for one week, to test the warnings?

## 15. When something is wrong (Chapter 32)

- Input. What went in?
- Action. What was run?
- Expected. What should have come out?
- Actual. What did come out?
- Every time, or once? The exact error text.
- The smallest example that still fails.
- The first stage where it goes wrong.
- The one cause, in one plain sentence.
- The test that will stay.

## 16. Permission audit and stop switch (Chapter 33)

For each automation and agent, every few months
- What can it reach, and does it still need all of it?
- When was the access last used?
- Who else has access, and do they have their own sign-in?
- What does it remember about me, and does any of it need clearing?
- Is there a spending limit and a usage warning?

The stop switch, in order of harm
- First, anything that can send, pay or publish, with the exact steps to pause it.
- Then anything that changes files.
- Then anything that only reads.
- How to check that it really stopped.
- What not to do in a hurry.
- Who knows where this page is, and the date of the last hospital test.

## 17. Acceptance test for an automation (Chapters 24, 27, 32 and 33)

Before you trust a new automation, or after you change one, run it on copies and check five things. Write the date and the result beside each.
- The original is safe. A dated copy exists, it opens, and the original files are unchanged.
- The fence held. Nothing outside the job's folder or account changed, and the canary is untouched.
- A success looks right. One normal run gives the result you checked by hand.
- A failure is noticed. One bad file, and one week with the schedule switched off, both reach you as a warning.
- Someone else can stop it. A person who didn't build it pauses it from the stop switch page, and it really stops, then restarts cleanly.

If any line fails, the automation isn't finished.

## Checking before you act

- Low stakes. Does the answer match the question, come in the form you asked for and give a clear next step?
- Medium stakes. Check dates, names, units, arithmetic, sources and whether anything was quietly assumed.
- High stakes, such as medical, legal, financial, employment, safety or identity. Use the AI's work as preparation only, and confirm with a qualified person or an authoritative source.
- Files changed by an agent. Check the folder itself, not just the report.

You can ask the assistant to review its own answer, but that isn't independent proof. The same model can repeat the same mistake in both the answer and the review. Check important claims against the original document, an official source, a calculation you do separately, or an accountable professional.
